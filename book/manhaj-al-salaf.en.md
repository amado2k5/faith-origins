Belief represents the foundations upon which any religion or divine or human system is built, and before we specifically study the Islamic belief, we must answer some questions about religion itself: What is the meaning of religion in language and terminology? Is religion an innate disposition or instinct, like other human instincts? And if it is an innate disposition, is any religion sufficient to satisfy that disposition? ## The Conventional Meaning

When we move to what religious scholars are concerned with from these linguistic meanings, we find that what is most important to them is the second and third meanings only, meaning that they study religion in terms of it being a psychological state involving submission and obedience to a higher being to whom worship and submission are due. Also, they study it in terms of it being a specific relationship with its own principles and rituals. Thus, the word "din" is intended in only two meanings in the interest of religious scholars:

One of them: the psychological state, which is submission and worship, what is usually expressed as "religiosity."

The second: the principles and rituals to which the individual or the group submits, what is usually expressed as "religion."

As for the first meaning, which is specific to the deity itself, it is outside the scope of their interest, as it is considered philosophical research, the domain of theology or theology and religious philosophy. This is what distinguishes the study of comparative religion.

From here, each one defined religion in the meaning that settled in his opinion regarding that origin. ## Worship

# 3 - Rituals and Symbols:
And indeed, all religions share the inclusion of specific rituals and symbols through which the believer expresses his reverence and submission to that sublime Being whom he worships and draws near to.

# 4 - Fear and Hope:
The believer in every religion, when he draws near to his deity through various rituals and acts of worship, does so in hope of His help and support and His pleasure, and out of fear of His punishment and anger. From these common elements, we can arrive at a complete definition of religion in its general sense. # The Religion and the Doctrine

The Church, considers any deviation from its teachings as blasphemy, falsehood, and disbelief in God and His Book. Therefore, every Christian must submit to the idea that the Holy Book contains the amount of knowledge that God has deemed appropriate for humans to know, and that everything it contains is the truth, regardless of whether it contradicts reason or experience. Thus, the Church forbade every Christian from expressing an opinion that contradicts the Holy Book, and instead required them to accept the interpretations of the Church's clergy and their explanations of it. So much so that one scholar was sentenced to death for stating that animals were killed before Adam's sin, and another scholar was imprisoned and tortured to death for saying that the rainbow is not a weapon in God's hand, as the Church claims, but rather a reflection of sunlight on raindrops.

Indeed, this stubbornness was merely an expression of the narrow-mindedness, fanaticism, and greed of religious leaders. They saw in scientific and intellectual freedom a threat to their authority and dominance, and thus resorted to all means of violence and cruelty to defend their power and interests. They established what became known as the Inquisition to suppress the advocates of the Scientific Enlightenment and to punish anyone who dared to challenge the Church's authority without mercy or compassion. It is enough for us to know the extent of the Church's brutality and savagery in suppressing the spirit of enlightenment, that the terrible Inquisition Courts, from their inception in 1481 AD to 1808 AD, sentenced nearly half a million people to death, burning many of them alive. This terrible court instilled such fear in people's hearts that anyone who had even a spark of intellectual light in their mind would imagine that the messenger of death was pursuing them, and that the executioner's sword would reach their head before any scientific idea could reach them. # Translation guide: Al-Milla wa al-Nihla (Arabic to English)

Rules every translator (human or model) follows, so a book translated in pieces reads as one voice.

## Principles
- **Faithful, complete, readable.** Translate every heading, paragraph, quotation and footnote. Do not summarise, skip, soften,
  sharpen or comment. The author's argument and tone are his; the translation does not endorse or correct them.
- Natural scholarly English, not word-for-word. Keep the author's sentence rhythm where it can be done without strain.
- The text is OCR from a scan. Silently repair obvious OCR slips (missing hamza, a swapped letter, a split word) when the
  meaning is clear from context. If a passage is truly unreadable, write `[illegible]` and keep going. Never invent text.
- Keep the Markdown structure: `#`/`##`/`###` headings, paragraphs, lists, `---` rules and `<sup>` as in the source.
- Footnotes: the source marks them `(١)`, `(٢)`…; write `(1)`, `(2)`… and keep each footnote text. Translate titles of cited
  Arabic works as `Transliterated Title (English gloss)` on first use in a footnote, e.g. `Al-Fitna al-Kubra (The Great Sedition)`.
- Numbers: Western digits. Hijri years as `AH 1409`, Gregorian years as is.

## Religious formulae
- `عليه السلام` after a prophet: `(peace be upon him)`. `ﷺ`: `(peace and blessings be upon him)`.
  `رضي الله عنه`: `(may God be pleased with him)`. `رحمه الله`: `(may God have mercy on him)`.
  Use them as the author does; do not drop or add them.
- Names: use the conventional English form: Moses, Jesus, Mary, Abraham, Paul, Muhammad, Abu Bakr, Uthman, Ali.

## Quotations from scripture
- **Qur'anic verses** (marked with ﴿ ﴾ or with a sura name and verse number in the footnote): translate faithfully into your own
  English, in a blockquote (`> `), followed by the reference as the source gives it, e.g. `(Al Imran 3:19)`. Do not copy any
  published translation word for word. Where the OCR garbled a verse, identify it from context and translate the verse itself.
  Add no commentary.
- **Bible and Talmud passages quoted in Arabic**: translate from the Arabic as quoted (do not substitute a published English
  Bible). Mark them as blockquotes.
- **Hadith**: translate the text; keep the narrator and source as given.

# Tawhid

Tawhid is used in two meanings:

## First Meaning:
Tawhid in the sense of devoting worship and obedience to Allah alone. In this sense, it means believing in Allah and His names and attributes as they are revealed in the divine revelation.

## Second Meaning:
Tawhid in the sense of a documented science, as it appears in the books of the science of theology. In this sense, it means the study of the essence of Allah, His names and attributes according to the principles of reason and contemplation.

We will deal with Tawhid in both meanings:

## First: Tawhid in the legal sense:

As we said, Tawhid in the legal sense means devoting worship to Allah alone. The religion of Islam is called the religion of Tawhid because Tawhid is to it what the heart is to the body or the center is to the circle. The foundation and essence of Islam is that Allah is one in His sovereignty and actions, with no partner; one in His essence and attributes, with no equal; and one in His divinity, with no rival and no deity other than Him: "Say, He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent" (1). This is the complete Tawhid, which includes the Tawhid of Lordship, the Tawhid of names and attributes, and the Tawhid of divinity, as they are mentioned in the revealed books, called for by the prophets and messengers, and believed in by the righteous predecessors and those who followed them in excellence. These types, namely the Tawhid of Lordship and the Tawhid of names and attributes, are mentioned in the complete Ikhlas.

# The Attributes and the Unity of Divinity

The attributes and the unity of divinity are interconnected, neither can be separated from the other. Whoever affirms one without affirming the other has an incomplete tawhid and a contradictory stance:

1. **The Unity of Lordship (Rububiyah):**

This means affirming the oneness of Allah in lordship, kingship, and management, and acknowledging that He, the Exalted, is the Lord of everything, its creator, sustainer, and guide. He is the One who brings life and causes death, who elevates and abases, who benefits and harms. His is the kingdom and His is the command. In His hand is the good, and He is over all things capable. The meaning is clear, and this type of tawhid is essential for the completeness of tawhid. However, affirming it alone is not sufficient to judge someone as a believer or a Muslim, if they do not also affirm its necessary companion, which is the unity of speech (Kubriyah).

The polytheists used to affirm this type of tawhid, as the Qur'an mentions about them in the verse: > "And if you ask them, 'Who created the heavens and the earth?' they will surely say, 'Allah.'" 2 - The Unity of Names and Attributes:

This means the exclusive perfection of God, which no one shares in any way, and the affirmation of what God has affirmed for Himself and what His Messenger has affirmed for Him of the most beautiful names and attributes, in a manner befitting His majesty, without negation, alteration, neglect, or representation. # The Science of Tawhid

## 1 - Definition

### In language:
It is the knowledge that something is one.

### In terminology:
It has many different definitions according to the different schools of thought. One of these definitions is the one given by the author of the book *Al-Mawaqi3*, who says: "It is a science by which one can establish religious beliefs upon others and obligate them to accept them by presenting arguments and refuting doubts." (19)

## 2 - Its Subject

It is far from being disputed. The subject of this science has also varied according to different opinions. Among these opinions is what Al-Bajuri said: "Its subject is the essence of Allah Most High, in terms of what is obligatory for Him, what is impossible, and what is permissible; and the essence of the messengers, obedience to them, and what is necessary and possible, inasmuch as it leads to the existence of the Creator and the acceptance of the unseen, in terms of belief in them."

## 3 - Its Goal

It is stated: "To rise above the depths of blind imitation and to guide those who seek guidance by clarifying the evidence and obligating it, and by establishing the evidence against those who oppose it, and by preserving the foundations of religion from being shaken by the doubts of the ignorant, and by building upon it the religious sciences, for it is their foundation and to it they return and from which they derive and their validity depends. This is the intention of the righteous and the belief, for by it the acceptance of deeds is hoped for, and the ultimate goal of all this is to attain the happiness of the two worlds." (20)

## 4 - Its Names

This science, although it deals with many issues other than the essence of Allah, is called the science of *tawhid* because the issue of oneness (*tawhid*) is the most noble and important of its subjects, so the science is named after its most noble subject.

# First: The View of Ibn Khaldun

He says: "The Legislator, when He commanded us to believe in this Creator, to Whom He has returned all actions and to Whom He has assigned them exclusively, and who has informed us that this belief is our salvation when we are present at death, did not inform us of the essence of this Creator, Who is worshipped, for He is beyond our comprehension and above our level. Therefore, He imposed upon us:

First: To believe in His transcendence in His essence, unlike the creatures, otherwise it would not be valid that He is their Creator, for there would be no distinction on this assumption.

Then, His transcendence from defects, otherwise He would resemble the creatures.

Then, His oneness in unity, otherwise creation would not be complete.

Then, to believe that He is knowledgeable, for through this, the actions are perfected, and this is a witness to the truth of the unity and the creation.

And that He is willing, otherwise none of the creatures would be chosen.

And that He is the Knower of everything existing, otherwise the will would be a created thing.

And that He will bring us back after death, to complete His care for existence, for if it were for a fleeting command, it would be in vain, so it is for eternal existence after death.

Then, to believe in the sending of the messengers for salvation from the hardship of this return, for their conditions differ between hardship and happiness, and to show the two paths, and that Paradise is for pleasure and Hell is for punishment.

These are the fundamental beliefs of faith, explained by their rational proofs, and their proofs from the Book and the Sunna are numerous. From those proofs, the predecessors took them, and the scholars were guided by them, and the imams verified them.

However, after that, there arose a dispute over the details of these beliefs, most of which were caused by ambiguous verses, which led to contention, debate, and reasoning with the intellect in addition to the narration, and thus the science of theology arose. This is because the Qur'an contains many verses describing the worshipped as absolutely transcendent, with clear indication without interpretation, and these verses are all explicit in their subject, and they are the basis for belief. And this was mentioned in the speech of the Legislator (peace be upon him) and the speech of the Companions.

# Imitation and Inquiry

We have previously defined belief as "certain knowledge conforming to reality arising from evidence," and we stated that this last condition, namely "arising from evidence," is a matter of contention among scholars, known among them as the issue of "imitation and inquiry." Before presenting their views on this issue, we believe it is necessary to highlight some points that aid in understanding this matter, which has been the subject of contention and debate among theologians.

First: The disagreement among scholars is regarding the knowledge of God and His attributes through rational evidence, that is, through reasoning and inquiry. As for knowledge in the sense of belief in Him, there is no disagreement among them that it is the first duty required of the responsible person, and that knowledge of it is an individual obligation, and ignorance of it is disbelief.

Second: There is no disagreement among them regarding the belief of a person whose belief is based on imitation that is accompanied by doubt or uncertainty, or is susceptible to doubt or uncertainty. For example, if his belief is based on the belief of the one he imitates, such that if the imitated person abandons belief, the imitator would also abandon belief. This kind of imitation is not considered valid, and the person is not considered a believer by consensus. Rather, the disagreement among them is regarding the belief of the imitator who is certain, that is, the one who does not abandon belief if the imitated person abandons belief.

Third: The disagreement regarding the belief of the imitator is only in terms of the rulings of the Hereafter and what is with God. As for the rulings of this world, mere acknowledgment is sufficient. Thus, whoever acknowledges the testimony and the pillars of Islam, the rulings of Muslims apply to him, and he is judged to be a Muslim and not judged to be a disbeliever, unless he does something that necessitates disbelief, such as worshipping an idol or something similar.

Fourth: There is no disagreement among scholars regarding the virtue of reasoning and inquiry. The entire Quran and Sunna call for knowledge, reasoning, and inquiry. Rather, the disagreement is regarding the subject of inquiry. The Salafis<sup>(1)</sup>

They hold that the subject of praiseworthy thought is the creation, not the Creator, as the religion has guided us with this principle: "Reflect upon the creation of God, and do not reflect upon His essence, lest you perish." As for knowledge of the Creator, its source is revelation, not reason, and the matter rests upon following, not innovation. As for the theologians, the subject of their inquiry includes both, the creation and the Creator.

# The Ruling on the Belief of the One Who Follows Others

As a result of their disagreement on the necessity of independent reasoning (*ijtihad*), they also differ in the ruling on the one who follows others completely, like the common people, with many opinions, including:

1. If one follows the Qur'an and the certain Sunna, then his belief is valid, because the certainty of the proofs is clear. But if one follows someone who is not certain, then his belief is not valid, due to the risk of error from someone who is not inerrant.  
   In this case, he is considered to be in error.

2. The one who follows others is a believer, but he becomes a sinner if he has the ability to reason and the conditions for it are present. If he does not have the ability, then he is not a sinner. The majority of the Ash'arites have returned to this opinion.  
   This is the opinion of the people of reason.

3. The one who follows others is a believer and not a sinner at all, whether he is capable of reasoning or not. According to this group, reasoning is a condition for perfection. Therefore, if someone has the ability to reason but does not do so, he has abandoned the preferable action, but he is not a sinner for leaving it.  
   If they stone the one who follows blindly, it is not justified.

4. It was also said that he is a disbeliever, because the first duty upon the responsible person is to know God, and knowledge of God cannot be achieved except through reasoning. Therefore, reasoning is obligatory, because whatever is necessary for fulfilling an obligation is itself obligatory.  
   This is an obligation in person.

These are the most important or summarized opinions of the scholars on the issue of following others and reasoning. # Islam and Faith

Know first that the discussion of faith and Islam here refers to faith and Islam that are considered religiously valid, leading to righteousness in this world and success in the Hereafter.

## Definition of Faith

Faith in language: general belief, as in the verse: > "And you are not a believer to us," (1)  
meaning one who affirms.

Religiously: belief in the Prophet and acceptance of everything he brought, and knowledge of the religion by necessity.  
The intended belief here is the belief that results in submission, obedience, compliance, and acceptance.  
It is not merely believing in the prophethood of Muhammad without submission and following what he brought from his Lord. Because the disbelievers, as the Qur'an describes them, were: > "who know him as they know their own children, and indeed, a group among them conceal the truth while they know it," (2) and among them are those who believe in his prophethood but only to the Arabs.

## Definition of Islam

# The Relationship Between Faith and Islam:

2. Indeed.

If Islam means obedience, submission, and acceptance of the laws and commands of Allah, then the Muslim cannot achieve this Islam in its perfection, except if he is certain in his heart of the existence of Allah and His oneness, and that there is none like Him in His divinity, lordship, and perfection. This certainty is the foundation upon which the ethics, behavior, and worldview of the Muslim are built, and it is what guides his steps on the straight path, whether in matters relating to his relationship with Allah, or with himself, or with others and all of existence. And this cannot be achieved by a person unless he knows the method of Allah in all of this and follows it, and is certain of the results of adhering to this method, whether in matters relating to his worldly life or to his afterlife. Therefore, he must believe in the messengers of Allah and His books and the Day of Judgment, and that the result of this belief and acting upon it is success and paradise, and neglecting it means loss and hellfire.

This is the meaning of faith: knowledge, understanding, and firm certainty in Allah and His method and His reward. The fruit of this knowledge is Islam, which is the application of certainty, making one obedient and submissive to Allah and following His law.

From this, you learn that the relationship between faith and Islam is the relationship between the result and the cause, or the effect and the effecting cause, or the relationship between the seed and the tree. Just as the existence of the tree cannot be imagined without the existence of its seed, so the true meaning of Islam cannot be imagined without the existence of faith. # First: The Name of Faith

There has been a great deal of difference of opinion regarding the name of faith, including the following views:

1. What Malik, ash-Shafi'i, Auza'i, and others among the hadith scholars held, is that faith is defined as belief in the heart, affirmation with the tongue, and action in the pillars. With this understanding, faith encompasses inner belief and outward compliance, that is, belief, speech, and action.

This is what the Mu'tazila and the Kharijites among the theologians held, except for the difference between them and the Salafis. The difference is that the Salafis consider one who falls short in action or commits a major sin without considering it permissible, meaning he believes it is permissible, to be a believing sinner, whereas the Mu'tazila consider him to be in a middle state, and the Kharijites consider him to be a disbeliever.

2. What al-Tha'alibi and others among the hadith and jurisprudence scholars held is that faith is belief and affirmation only, whereas action is not included in the essence of faith. In reality, there is no difference between this view and the first view of the Salafis, because both views agree regarding one who falls short in action, as both consider him to be a believing sinner. The difference is that the first group looked at faith in its highest limit, whereas the second group looked at it in its lowest limit.

3. What al-Maturidi and Abu Hanifa held in a narration attributed to him is that faith is inner belief only, whereas affirmation and action are outside its definition. What is reported from Abu Hanifa is that "Faith is belief in what the Messenger necessarily brought, detailed in what is known in detail and general in what is known in general. As for affirmation with the tongue and action, they are not included in the essence of faith."

This view has been refuted, as it is known from religion with necessary knowledge that one who believes in his heart, but does not affirm with his tongue when capable, and does not perform any of the obligations, and does not love God and His Messenger, is not a believer.

# The Qur'anic Method in Demonstrating the Existence of God

Before presenting the Qur'anic method in demonstrating the existence of God, we would like to point out two matters.

The first matter: those who deny the existence of God fall into two categories of people:

One category denies the existence of God altogether; these are the materialists or, as they are called in the books of theology, the *zahiriyya*. The Qur'an adopts a method towards these people that aims to prove the existence of God through contemplation of the universe and the self.

The second category consists of those who deny the worship of God, meaning they deny His exclusivity in worship; these are the polytheists. This category is the majority of humanity, on various levels. The Qur'an adopts a method towards these people that aims to refute their claim by proving the inability of all others besides God and their need for Him.

The second matter: the Qur'anic method in demonstrating the existence of God differs from the method of the theologians and others, in that the Qur'an is accustomed to presenting verses of creation and lordship, which are not disputed by anyone and are accepted by all as a proof and direct evidence of the existence of God and His attributes. The Qur'an does not construct its proofs logically from a minor and major premise and a proposition and conclusion in the manner of the theologians, nor does it use the terminology of philosophers such as essence, accident, possible, necessary, and similar terms. Rather, it relies in its proofs on the sound nature, direct observation, the obviousness of reason and feeling, and the scholars and philosophers, like the ignorant and the common people, are all capable of being affected by the Qur'anic proofs, according to their different capacities and levels of understanding, because the Qur'an does not address scholars or philosophers alone, nor was it sent solely for the common people, but rather it was sent as guidance for all humanity to the truth and goodness, and thus scholars believed in it as did the common people.

The Qur'an is the path of the philosophers in reasoning, but only a few believe in it.

# The Impact of Belief in God on the Life of a Muslim

Belief in God is more than an absolute right belonging to God; it is an obligation that has an infinite dimension in the ethics of the Muslim, his happiness, and his liberation from submission to self-imposed standards, however great their worldly power may be. It empties his heart of desires and worldly inclinations, worries and sorrows, and fills it with the light of truth, certainty, security, tranquility, hope, and expectation. The fruits of belief in God in the life of a Muslim are numerous, too many for any human to count, among them:

First: The security of the individual and the society:

As for the security of the individual and his spiritual peace, the sorrow of the modern human being and what he suffers from in terms of fragmentation, anxiety, worry, sorrow, fear, and depression, in reality, stems from his loss of security and psychological peace, due to the internal conflicts within himself and his loss of the highest ideal that unifies his energies in one direction, which caused in him the feeling of alienation and loss. There is no cure for all the psychological illnesses he suffers from, which have worsened to an epidemic level that threatens the existence of humanity and its civilization, except by unifying his self and his goals, but this unification cannot be achieved in a correct way except when the soul believes in an absolute ideal of perfection in essence, attributes, and actions, which is its goal in perfection and is itself the motive for it to strive for perfection. And since God, may He be exalted, is the only perfection in essence, attributes, and actions, He is the only one worthy of the soul turning to Him with sincerity, love, and obedience, and He is the highest ideal who alone is capable of unifying the human self and directing its energies toward perfection. # Fate and Destiny

Belief in fate is the sixth of the doctrines of faith, and it is a criterion for the depth of a Muslim's belief in God and a witness to the sincerity of his worship and trust in Him. We will discuss fate and destiny from the following aspects: its meaning, the origin of the debate around it, the stance of the early scholars toward it, and the effect of belief in it.

## First: Its Meaning

The scholars differed in its meaning into two opinions:

The first opinion: They defined destiny as: The creation of things by Allah in a specific measure that Allah willed and exalted. With this meaning, it relates to the attribute of power.

As for fate, they defined it as: The will of Allah for things in the eternal past, as it is and will remain, and with this meaning, it relates to the attribute of knowledge.

The second opinion: It holds that destiny is: The determination by Allah in the eternal past of every created thing in the way in which it exists, whether good or evil, beneficial or harmful, and so on. With this meaning, it relates to the attribute of knowledge.

As for fate, it is the creation of things by Allah with added precision and perfection. With this meaning, it relates to the attribute of power. As in the verse: "(Who created and proportioned; and Who measured and guided)" (1)

If they had done so, they would not have been believers in their prophet, but rather they would have submitted, surrendered, and obeyed, and whatever wisdom they knew, they acted upon it, and whatever was hidden from them, they did not delay their submission and surrender because of it, nor did they consider it part of their duty. The first rank of honoring a command is to believe in it, then to be resolute in obeying it, then to hasten to it and take the initiative in it, and to beware of obstacles and deterrents, then to exert effort and sincerity in fulfilling it in the best ways, then to do it because it is commanded, without delaying its fulfillment until one knows its wisdom, for if its wisdom becomes apparent, one does it, and if not, one leaves it, for this contradicts submission and undermines obedience. # Issue

And when the Muslims were occupied with what God wanted from them, not with what He wanted for them, they prevailed in the world, and the belief in divine decree was a driving force for them toward progress, advancement, and dominance through justice and fairness. Thus, when belief in divine decree takes hold in the heart and consciousness of a Muslim, it becomes a driving force pushing him toward his goals and toward action, earning, and initiative, and toward removing all obstacles that stand between him and progress, prosperity, and dominance. It fills his heart with tranquility, confidence, determination, and boldness, because of his belief that nothing will befall him except what God has written for him. So the mighty hand of destiny becomes: 

> Rustam, the Persian commander, met an Arab from the vanguard of the Muslim army. He asked him, "What has brought you out of your homes?" The Arab Muslim replied, "We did not come out for this world, but we were weak and God made us strong, and we were astray and God guided us. Our Lord commanded us to convey the message. If you enter into what we have entered, then we and you are equal. Otherwise, the sword is between us and you." Rustam said, "Look at these armies," and the man looked at them with a sneer. Then he said, "O man, you are not fighting people, but you are fighting destiny. We are the decree of God, and He has placed us over you." (14)

After the Muslims became occupied with what God wanted for them rather than what He wanted from them, this flame in their hearts was extinguished and became ashes. The belief in destiny changed from a supporting and driving force into an opposing and contradictory force, and became one of the factors of despair, inactivity, and hopelessness.

And we ask God to return to us what made our beginning good, and our last prayer is that all praise is due to God, Lord of the worlds.
